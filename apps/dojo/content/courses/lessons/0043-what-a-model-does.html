Before you build a hint feature, see the machine it runs on. A language model doesn’t “answer questions”: it scores every possible next token, and a few lines of code turn those scores into one choice. Today you write those lines, and every word you’ll ever get from a model makes more sense.

Why this matters

This week CodeArena gets an AI hint for failed submissions, on a model you host yourself. Every decision this week (the temperature, the prompt, why evals run several times, why RAG works at all) follows from the loop in this lesson.

1. Text in, tokens in

A model doesn’t read characters or words. A tokenizer first cuts the text into tokens: frequent words become one token, rarer ones several pieces, and each token is an id in a fixed vocabulary of tens of thousands of entries. off-by-one may be four tokens; the is one. Token counts are what limits, prices and speeds are measured in, which is why Ollama reports prompt_eval_count and eval_count, not characters (Ollama API).

3Blue1Brown’s chapter on GPT shows this step, and what the network then does with each token, in about half an hour of animation (But what is a GPT?).

2. One token at a time

Given the tokens so far, the network computes one number per vocabulary token: a logit, a raw score for “this comes next”. Then:

  1. softmax turns the logits into probabilities: exponentiate each, divide by the sum (softmax function);
  2. a decoding rule picks one token from that distribution;
  3. the chosen token is appended to the input, and the whole thing runs again.

That loop, repeated until a stop token or a length limit, is all “generation” is. Two consequences you’ll meet all week: the model has no memory between calls (everything it uses must be in the input, up to its context window), and its knowledge stops where its training data stopped. Both are why RAG exists: you put the missing knowledge into the input.

3. Choosing the next token

logits  [3.0, 2.0, 1.0]
softmax [0.67, 0.24, 0.09]          # temperature 1
softmax [0.87, 0.12, 0.02]          # temperature 0.5: sharper
softmax [0.51, 0.31, 0.19]          # temperature 2: flatter
  • Greedy: always the largest logit. Deterministic, sometimes repetitive.
  • Temperature: divide every logit by T before softmax. Below 1 the distribution gets sharper, above 1 flatter; as T goes to 0, sampling becomes greedy (Hugging Face: how to generate).
  • Top-k: keep the k likeliest tokens, renormalise, sample.
  • Top-p (nucleus): keep the smallest set of likeliest tokens whose probabilities add up to p. Few tokens when the model is sure, many when it isn’t.

All of these are options of the request, not of the model: Ollama’s /api/chat takes temperature, top_k and top_p in its options (Ollama API).

5. What this means for CodeArena

  • A hint wants a low temperature. You want the most likely explanation, not a creative one. Start at 0 or close to it.
  • Even at 0, don’t expect identical runs forever. Batching and hardware make generation slightly nondeterministic in practice, and you’ll change models and prompts. That’s why lesson 46’s evals run each case several times and report a rate.
  • The model knows nothing about your submission unless the prompt carries it: the problem, the verdict, the failing test, the code, and, later, your notes.
